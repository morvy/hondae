Dashboard guide

Pictures from a real drive replayed through the app: 3 November 2026, 8 °C, heater on, 30 minutes in. Each widget at the size it gets when added; tap one for full size.

Reading a tile

Name, the value now, its unit, and a small line: lowest / average / highest since the app was opened. -- is no reading yet: not answered, the trip is too short, or it needs the car to move. A grey tile in the editor is one your adapter cannot deliver.

How often each reading updates

About every 0.2 sThe battery's main reply (ECU 01): POWER, CURRENT, PACK VOLTS, SPEED, WEAK CELL, BMS SOC, CELL HIGH / LOW / SPREAD, PACK TEMP, 12V, POWER / REGEN LIMIT, TORQUE, CABIN, CELL R MAX, INSULATION.
About every 6 sECU 15's state reply: CHARGE, AUX LOAD, CLIMATE, 12V LOAD, PACK HEAT, SOC SPREAD.
About every 20 sThe slow round: HEALTH, CHARGER TEMP, MAINS, STATION LIMIT (ECU 15), ODOMETER, OUTSIDE, RANGE CAR (ECU 60).
About every 1 sThe phone's GPS: ALTITUDE, GRADE, and the distance behind BEST / WORST MINUTE and Load analysis.

The editor shows the same thing when you pick a reading: "every cycle" or "1 in N". Adding tiles costs nothing; the app asks the car for everything it knows how to read either way.

What that means side by side

  • Calculated readings move at their slowest input. ENERGY LEFT follows CHARGE, so it steps every few seconds while POWER moves continuously. RANGE and USING blend fast power with distance, so they drift rather than jump.
  • Fast and slow tiles disagree for a moment. Switch the heater on and POWER rises at once, while CLIMATE and AUX LOAD catch up within about 6 seconds. That lag is the car's reply rate, not a fault.
  • The 20 s readings barely matter on a drive: health, the odometer and the outside temperature change slowly anyway.
  • The Battery and Tyres pages take the link while they are open, to sample cells or wheels as fast as possible. Dashboard tiles that are not on the same reply pause until you leave the page.

The dash and the app side by side

  • Dash 52 %, CHARGE 51.6 %. The same figure: the app reads the car's own number with two decimals; the dash rounds it and updates on its own schedule. CHARGE refreshes about every 6 s, so a point apart for a few seconds is normal.
  • Dash 15 %, WEAK CELL 8 %. Near empty the car re-bases its figure onto the weakest cell, so the dash can drop several points at once. CHARGE turns amber and says what it may fall to before that happens.
  • Dash range 84 km, RANGE AVG 50 km. The car's estimate (RANGE CAR) changes slowly and leans on its own history; the app divides the charge left by how this drive has actually gone. On a cold, short trip with the heater on the app reads lower; on a long steady drive the two come together.
  • The charger billed more kWh than ENERGY LEFT went up. On AC about 7 % is lost in the on-board charger, and the battery gives back about 5 % less than it took in, so a 0 to 100 % charge here (about 25 kWh to drive on) meters roughly 28 to 29 kWh at the wall.
  • The speedometer reads a little above SPEED. Car speedometers may read high but never low, so the dash is usually a few percent over the wheel speed the app shows.

Battery health

Turn on Settings › Pages › Cell page; it opens from the battery in the top bar. Pictures: a real drive replayed.

Swipe one page right from the dashboard, or tap the battery in the top bar. Across the top are HEALTH, the pack's voltage and temperature, and the highest cell, the lowest cell and the gap between them, the same readings as the tiles. Under them are the six module temperatures, then all 96 cells, then the pack's health over time.

The cell grid

Eight cells to a row, numbered from the top left. Three buttons under the grid switch what each cell shows.

  • VOLTS: which cell is highest and lowest right now. Blue is the lowest, red the highest, and both change with the load. On a healthy pack all 96 are within a few millivolts of each other, so the colours rank the cells; they do not judge them.
  • MILLIOHMS: each cell's resistance, and this one is a verdict. Green is normal, under 10 % above the middle of your own pack. Amber, 10 to 25 % above, is worth watching. Deep orange, 25 to 100 %, is a warning. Red, resistance doubled, is the published end-of-life line for a cell. The button stays grey until the current has moved by 20 A; a minute of ordinary driving with the page open does it, standing still never will. The whole pack reads higher in the cold and lower when warm, so compare a cell with its neighbours today, not with last week. Cells 9, 21, 33 and every twelfth after them can read high together while the current changes, mostly in the cold. That comes from how the car measures them, so they are not flagged; one of them high on its own still is.
  • AH: what each cell holds, as the battery has learned it. Around 40 to 42 Ah a cell. The smallest cell sets what the whole pack can hold. Up to a few percent between smallest and largest is normal: 0.7 % in the picture. Compare the cells with each other rather than reading the number as the pack's size; that is what HEALTH is for.

Every cell suddenly reads far less in AH

Now and then, most often after a drive that takes the battery low, all 96 cells drop together, on one car from about 41 Ah to 23 Ah, and stay there for a day or two before coming back on their own. The battery is re-learning its cells. It is not a fault and HEALTH does not move with it. Wait until the figures have settled before comparing cells.

Health over time

HEALTH is saved with each charge, and the line shows how it has stepped down. Under it, Measured capacity is what your own charges put in, scaled to 0 to 100 % on the dash: the middle value of every saved charge that covered at least 30 points and was read from start to finish. It is the part of the pack you can drive on, so compare it with 28.5 kWh × HEALTH ÷ 100, not with the 35.5 kWh of the whole pack. Expect it a few percent above that: a charge puts in a little more than driving takes back out. Until one charge qualifies it says what it needs.

While the page is open

  • The dashboard may pause. All 96 cells come in one large reply, so with the drive log off the other readings wait until you leave the page. With the drive log on, everything keeps updating and the cells refresh less often instead. The Tyres page works the same way.
  • You do not have to watch it. Whenever the cells are read, a cell crossing into a band writes a line into the car log naming it, so a drive leaves a record with nobody looking.
  • "Charge went while parked". If the charge fell more than 1.5 points while the car did not move, the page says so once, at the top, until you dismiss it. Climate left on, a timer or an open door are the usual causes; cold alone can move the gauge a point or two overnight. If it keeps happening with nothing switched on, have the car looked at.

Charging

Turn on Settings › Pages › Charging page; it opens from the bolt in the top bar. It records every charge, open or not, and keeps the last one to look at afterwards. Pictures: invented data.

During a charge

  • The ring is the charge on the dash's scale. Beside it: CONNECTOR, AC or DC as the car reports it, POWER going into the battery, and ADDED, the kWh put in so far.
  • VOLTAGE, CURRENT, AVERAGE: the pack's voltage, the charging current, and the kWh added divided by the time so far.
  • TIME TO 80 % and ENERGY TO 80 % (100 % once you are past 80) come from how fast the charge rose over the last 5 minutes. For the first 2 minutes they say estimating. The energy is what the battery takes in; on AC the charger bills about 7 % more.
  • "The car is warming the battery": part of the power is heating the pack, not filling it. Nothing to do. "Cold pack": the coldest module is at 10 °C or below, so expect a slower charge until it warms.
  • Under the curve: CELL HIGH, CELL LOW, PACK TEMP and the six module temperatures, the hottest in amber. A weak cell shows first during a charge, running higher than the rest as the pack fills.

The power curve

Power into the battery against charge time, or against charge level with SOC. A solid line is what the app read; a dashed line is a stretch where nobody was reading, filled in at an estimated power. A dropout of up to 5 minutes, or an app restart, carries on the same charge. A longer gap, or a charge that starts lower than the last one ended, starts a new one.

After a charge

Not charging, the page shows the last charge and when it ended: DURATION, PEAK and ADDED. With the car not connected it says it is not reading the car.

Saved charges

Turn on Settings › Pages › Save charging sessions and each charge is kept as its own file, listed under the curve: when it ran, the charge from and to, minutes, kWh, average and peak kW. Tap one to draw it on the curve, tap again to go back. Edit takes what the charger's receipt says; boxes left empty keep their value or follow from the others. Share sends the file, Delete removes it for good. Includes estimated charging interval means part of it was not read.

"Unfinished charge"

The car finished charging while the adapter was asleep, so the log stops below where the car is now. If the charger told you when it finished, its peak or the kWh, type them in and press Finish with this; all three are optional. Discard deletes that charge's log.

Tyres

Turn on Settings › Pages › Tyres page; it opens from the tyre in the top bar and takes effect on the next connection. Picture: a real drive replayed.

The Honda e has no pressure sensors. A tyre losing air rolls on a slightly smaller radius and turns faster than the other three, and the car reports each wheel's speed. The page watches for that, the way the car's own deflation warning does.

Getting started

  • Set all four tyres cold with a gauge to the pressures on the driver's door pillar, enter them, and press These are set - start learning.
  • Drive with the page open on the phone. It reads the wheels only while the page is on screen, and not while a car screen is showing the dashboard. About ten minutes of steady driving between 30 and 130 km/h teaches it these tyres; the page shows how far it is, and a short drive carries on where the last stopped.
  • "Not counting right now" names why a moment is skipped: speed outside 30-130 km/h, hard acceleration or regen, braking, or a corner.
  • "No wheel speeds yet" after switching the page on: disconnect and connect once.

Reading it

  • Each tyre shows the pressure it would take to turn that fast, with a ±: the real value is inside it about 19 times in 20. Inside its ± it reads unchanged.
  • Amber is 10 % down, red 20 % down. Go and check that tyre with a gauge.
  • TREND, PER DRIVE: one point a drive for the last 60, the pressure you set dashed. A slow leak is one line sinking while the other three stay level.
  • After pumping the tyres, press I've set the pressures again and enter them, so it learns them afresh.

What it cannot see

All four going down together, since each tyre is compared with the others. It tells you to look, it is not a gauge: check the pressures by hand now and then.

Drive log

Turn on Settings › Pages › Drive log and allow location; it opens from the road in the top bar. Picture: invented data.

Each drive is saved as a GPX file, written as you drive, so it is complete even if the app closes on the way. A new file starts with each drive and after a charge; if the car or the adapter picks up again within five minutes, the same file carries on.

The list

  • By month, newest first, with the month's distance, energy, consumption and number of drives.
  • Each drive: when it started and ended, how long it took, the distance and the average speed, both including stops, as the car's trip meter does. Then the consumption, the energy that left the pack and what regen put back.
  • A dash means the drive cannot say: consumption needs at least a kilometre, and the oldest drives have no used and regen figures.
  • RECORDING marks the drive being written. It cannot be shared or deleted until it ends.
  • Share sends the file to Drive, a mail or a map app; Delete removes it for good.

The files

Each point carries the car's speed, power, current, pack voltage, the weakest cell's charge, energy used and regenerated, consumption, road grade and altitude; a map app ignores all that and draws the route. Extended driving data adds the dash's charge, odometer, temperatures, range, battery health and all 96 cell voltages, about 10 MB an hour. Open a file in the GPX Player to replay it. Drives stay on the phone until you delete them, unless Delete old logs from the phone is on in Settings.

Settings

The gear in the top bar, or the last page when swiping. Everything here is off until you turn it on, unless it says otherwise.

The first time

  1. Plug the OBD adapter in under the dashboard and switch the car on.
  2. Adapter › Choose adapter and pick it from the list. Most adapters do not need pairing in Android first.
  3. Tap Not connected at the top left to connect.
  4. File storage › Choose folder, so drives and charges are kept where you can find them.
  5. Turn on the pages you want under Pages.

Adapter and connecting

The app reads the car through a Bluetooth OBD-II adapter (an ELM327 type) in the car's diagnostic socket. Choose adapter lists adapters already paired with the phone (marked Classic SPP) and Bluetooth LE adapters found nearby (marked BLE). Most BLE adapters never pair: pick them here, not in Android's Bluetooth settings.

The dot at the top left shows the link; tap it to connect or disconnect.

It saysWhat it means

DisconnectedThe link failed. See below.
  • Connect when the app opens connects every time you open the app, as if you had tapped it, except after you pressed Disconnect, until the app is closed and opened again.
  • Connect when the car does (Android 12 or newer) starts reading when the phone joins the car's Bluetooth, with the app closed. Pair the phone with the car as for calls, press Link car, pick the car as the phone's Bluetooth list names it and confirm. To have the route in the drive log on those drives, press Allow location all the time and choose that. Reading stops ten minutes after the car goes to sleep, so neither battery is drained overnight.
  • The adapter talks to one app at a time. If Car Scanner, ABRP's own OBD link or the adapter's app has it, this app gets nothing.
  • It finds the adapter but never connects: unplug the adapter for about ten seconds and plug it back in. Some adapters stop accepting anyone after a connection was cut off, while still showing up in the list.

Units

Consumption in kWh/100km (the default), km/kWh, mi/kWh or kWh/100mi; the last two also switch distances and speeds to miles. It applies everywhere, except a dashboard tile that has been given its own unit in the editor.

File storage and Google Drive

  • Choose folder picks a folder on the phone for drives (GPX) and saved charges. A file is written privately while it is recorded, then copied into the folder and checked once it is finished. Without a folder, logs stay in the app's private storage; you can still share them, but a file manager will not show them. Open folder shows it, Move logs brings older private logs across.
  • Upload logs to Google Drive copies every log in the folder to a "Honda e logs" folder in your Drive, so they survive losing or resetting the phone. It needs the folder above. The app can see only the files it puts there, nothing else in your Drive. Upload to Drive now sends whatever is waiting.
  • Delete old logs from the phone removes drives and charges over 30 days old each time the app starts. With Drive upload on, only once they are in Drive, which keeps its copies; with it off they are gone for good.

Config backup

Export config saves the app's settings to a file; Import config reads one back, checks it and asks before applying it. Use it when moving to a new phone or reinstalling.

  • In the file: dashboard layouts, theme, units, car colour, the pages and logs you turned on, keep-screen-on, connect-when-the-app-opens, the ABRP settings and the vehicle information sheet.
  • Not in it: the adapter, the linked car and the storage folder. Pick those again after importing; after a reinstall, choose the folder first.
  • Keep the file private. It holds your ABRP token and key and the car's VIN.

Screen and theme

  • Keep the screen on while the app is open. The phone will not sleep in a pocket either.
  • Theme: Dark (the default), Light or Auto. Auto follows the phone's own light and dark setting, and on the car's screen it follows the car, which knows when the headlamps are on.

Pages

Each page adds an icon to the top bar and a stop when swiping, in this order.

  • Cell page (Battery health). While it is open the battery is the only thing asked for, so the dashboard's own readings pause, unless the car's screen is showing them.
  • Charging page (Charging). Costs nothing: it is built from readings the app takes anyway.
  • Save charging sessions keeps each charge as its own CSV file, listed on the Charging page. Works with or without that page.
  • Tyres page (Tyres). Reads the wheel speeds only while the page is open; takes effect on the next connection.
  • Drive log (Drive log) records each drive as a GPX file. Needs location allowed. Extended driving data adds the dash's charge, odometer, temperatures, range, battery health and all 96 cell voltages to each point: about 10 MB an hour, from the next app start.

ABRP

A Better Route Planner plans a trip with charging stops from your car's charge and consumption. Normally it guesses them from its own model. With Send live data on, this app feeds it the car's real readings, so the plan follows how the drive is actually going.

  1. In ABRP, open your car's settings and copy its personal telemetry token (the generic or "Other" live-data option).
  2. Create a free telemetry API key at abetterrouteplanner.com/home/app/api-keys/telemetry.
  3. Paste both into Settings › ABRP and turn Send live data on.

What is sent, every 5 seconds while the app is connected and the car is awake: charge, power, speed, position, heading and altitude (when location is allowed), whether it is charging, AC or DC, parked or driving, battery health and capacity, pack voltage and current, battery and outside temperature, and the odometer. Only to ABRP, and only while the switch is on.

  • Is it working? The car log says whether ABRP accepted the data, and when it refuses, ABRP's reason: a wrong token or a missing key.
  • Use this or ABRP's own OBD connection, not both. The adapter talks to one app at a time.

Vehicle information

The car's identity as its modules report it: the VIN, each module's part, software and serial numbers, and the battery's health and capacity. Press Read from car once while connected; the sheet is kept afterwards. Export as .txt saves all of it, for a dealer or your own records. Export anonymised .txt keeps each module's software version and the VIN with the car's serial starred out, enough to tell the model year but not the car: the one to post on a forum.

Help

Questions and answers is the in-app help. The ? beside a page's title opens it at that page's answer.

Decoded, not official

Every reading in the app is decoded by the app itself from what the car sends, worked out from logs of real drives and charges, not from Honda's documentation. A value can be off, mean something slightly different from its label, or simply be wrong. Treat it as a guide: the car's own displays and warnings come first, and anything that matters for safety or a repair is for a Honda dealer to check.
