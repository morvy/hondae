Dashboard guide

Pictures from a real drive replayed through the app: 3 November 2026, 8 °C, heater on, 30 minutes in. Each widget at the size it gets when added; tap one for full size.

Reading a tile

Name, the value now, its unit, and a small line: lowest / average / highest since the app was opened (turn it off in Settings › Look › Theme and colours › Min / avg / max line). -- is no reading yet: not answered, the trip is too short, or it needs the car to move. A grey tile in the editor is one your adapter cannot deliver.

How often each reading updates

About every 0.2 sThe battery's main reply (ECU 01): POWER, CURRENT, PACK VOLTS, SPEED, WEAK CELL, BMS SOC, CELL HIGH / LOW / SPREAD, PACK TEMP, 12V, POWER / REGEN LIMIT, TORQUE, CABIN.
About every 6 sECU 15's state reply: CHARGE, AUX LOAD, CLIMATE, 12V LOAD, PACK HEAT, SOC SPREAD.
About every 20 sThe slow round: CHARGER TEMP, MAINS, STATION LIMIT (ECU 15), ODOMETER, OUTSIDE, RANGE CAR (ECU 60).
About every 1 sThe phone's GPS: ALTITUDE, GRADE, and the distance behind BEST / WORST MINUTE and Load analysis.

The editor shows the same thing when you pick a reading: "every cycle" or "1 in N". Adding tiles costs almost nothing: the app asks the car for everything it knows how to read, except a few readings (the accelerator, the brake lamps, the car's own consumption, the drivetrain rows) that it asks for only while a tile or the drive log uses them. With Settings › Look › Prioritize screen data on, it asks only for what the open screen shows.

What that means side by side

  • Calculated readings move at their slowest input. ENERGY LEFT follows CHARGE, so it steps every few seconds while POWER moves continuously. RANGE and USING blend fast power with distance, so they drift rather than jump.
  • Fast and slow tiles disagree for a moment. Switch the heater on and POWER rises at once, while CLIMATE and AUX LOAD catch up within about 6 seconds. That lag is the car's reply rate, not a fault.
  • The 20 s readings barely matter on a drive: health, the odometer and the outside temperature change slowly anyway.
  • The Battery health and TPMS pages take the link while they are open, to sample cells or wheels as fast as possible. Dashboard tiles that are not on the same reply pause until you leave the page, unless the drive log is on. With Prioritize screen data on, every screen reads only what it shows, and an (i) in the top bar says when other readings are held back.

The dash and the app side by side

  • Dash 52 %, CHARGE 51.6 %. The same figure: the app reads the car's own number with two decimals; the dash rounds it and updates on its own schedule. CHARGE refreshes about every 6 s, so a point apart for a few seconds is normal.
  • Dash 15 %, WEAK CELL 8 %. Near empty the car re-bases its figure onto the weakest cell, so the dash can drop several points at once. CHARGE turns amber and says what it may fall to before that happens.
  • Dash range 84 km, RANGE AVG 50 km. The car's estimate (RANGE CAR) changes slowly and leans on its own history; the app divides the charge left by how this drive has actually gone. On a cold, short trip with the heater on the app reads lower; on a long steady drive the two come together.
  • The charger billed more kWh than ENERGY LEFT went up. On AC about 7 % is lost in the on-board charger, and the battery gives back about 5 % less than it took in, so a 0 to 100 % charge here (about 25 kWh to drive on) meters roughly 28 to 29 kWh at the wall.
  • The speedometer reads a little above SPEED. Car speedometers may read high but never low, so the dash is usually a few percent over the wheel speed the app shows.

Tile designer

Every tile can be moved, resized, renamed, recoloured, and given limits and a graph. The pencil in the top bar arranges the dashboard; the handle on a tile opens its designer. Pictures: a real drive replayed.

Arranging the dashboard

The pencil in the top bar puts the dashboard in Arrange mode. Each tile gets three handles.

  • Move: hold a tile to pick it up, then drop it on another.
  • Resize: pull the handle in the bottom-right corner.
  • Remove: the red cross at the top right.
  • Design: the handle in the bottom-left corner opens the tile designer.

Add lists every reading and widget by topic. The grey figure on a chip is how often its reading is asked again; placing more tiles does not slow anything down. A dimmed chip is a reading past what your adapter returns: it reads as a dash until a roomier adapter is chosen. Reset puts the default dashboard back (tap it twice). Portrait and landscape are arranged separately: turn the phone to arrange the other.

Change

Change at the top of the designer swaps the tile for another reading or widget, with a search. The size stays; a reading changed to another keeps its label, colours, limits and graph.

The marks on a chip say what it draws: several figures, a chart or a picture. The grey figure is how often it refreshes.

Size

The designer shows the tile live at the top, as it will look on the dashboard, with the tabs under it. What you set is for this tile only: two USING tiles can show different figures in different colours. Done keeps the changes, Cancel drops them.

Width and Height in cells, up to the whole row and six down, or a preset. Some tiles show more with room: DRIVETRAIN, COOLANT, 12V SOC, POWER USE and USING on Both list their other figures beside or under the main one on a tile four wide, or three wide and taller. Those tiles also get their own layout, label and figure sizes on the Text tab.

Text

  • Label: rename the tile.
  • Value size in seven steps, the middle one the usual size, and weight from thin to bold. The number never outgrows the tile.
  • Decimals: 0, 1 or 2.
  • Unit: beside the number, under it, at the bottom, or off.
  • Description line: the small min / avg / max line under the number.

Units and basis

  • Units: this tile's own, whatever Settings say: km/kWh on one consumption tile and kWh/100km on another, or PS for power.
  • Basis, on RANGE and USING: which figure the tile shows. USING can show Now (the last five kilometres), Trip average, Since charge, Car (the car's own instant figure, as its dashboard gauge shows it), Instant (power ÷ speed, negative while regenerating) or Both (Now, with the others beside it on a wide tile).

Colours

Pick a colour for the label, the value, the unit and the description line, separately for the dark and the light theme. Tap a circle to pick; the small dot on it is the theme colour it replaces.

Some tiles have their own colours here: REGEN / BRAKES its lamps, POWER USE coloured or white figures, the Car widget your paint.

Limits

Add a limit below or above a number, in Warning amber, Danger red or a colour you pick. While the reading is past it, the number turns that colour (and the bar, on a tile with a bar). With several, the most extreme one wins. A limit here replaces the tile's own warning colour.

On REGEN / BRAKES the limits are where each lamp lights: the motor torque for regen, the brake pressure for the brakes (2 bar; under that the sensor reads noise).

Graph

  • Placement: a slim graph above or below the number, a tall bar left or right of it, or the graph in place of the number, which then sits small beside the label.
  • Kind: a horizontal or vertical bar for where the reading stands in its range, bars or a line for its recent history. Some readings can also take a widget's drawing: the power bar, load per minute, the drive graph, next speed, the range curve or the range bar.
  • Style: plain, steps (fainter toward the empty end) or a scale in the power colours.
  • Refresh and points: seconds between samples and how many are drawn. The graph uses what the app already reads and asks the car for nothing more.
  • Range: Auto, or a fixed min and max.
  • More bars: up to three more readings as bars beside this one, each in its own colour and range: power, torque and the accelerator side by side.

Widgets keep their own options on the same tabs: the power bar's hill split, aux and colour (As now or Stages), how many minutes the load chart and the drive graph hold, the speed limits Next speed costs out.

Battery health

Turn on Settings › Pages › Battery health; it appears as the battery icon in the bottom bar. Four tabs across the top: OVERVIEW, CELLS, HISTORY and 12 V. Pictures: a real drive replayed.

Tap the ? beside a title for the in-app help. Every tab ends with a NOTES bar that opens numbered explanations; the small (1), (2) on a card are the notes it refers to.

Overview

  • SOH is the pack's health: what it can hold now, in % of the rated 100.5 Ah, with the Ah beside it and how far it has moved since the first saved charge. LINE L and LINE R under it give each of the pack's two halves the same way.
  • PACK TEMP and INSULATION: the warmest sensor, and the resistance between the high-voltage system and the car's body in kΩ, with the number of checks the battery has made.
  • POWER LIMITS: the most the battery will give (DRIVE) and take back (REGEN) right now, as a short burst (PEAK) and what it can keep up (STEADY). Both drop when the pack is cold, drive near empty, regen near full. When every module reports reduced power the page says so; steady regen comes back as the pack warms.
  • PACK THERMAL: the coolant temperatures, the pump and the radiator fan. A flame and a heater figure appear while the car is heating the pack.
  • WEAK CELLS names the cells that balance more than a typical cell, one list per line. Tap it to see them on the cell map (CELLS › BALANCING). Until the car's counters have been read it says to open the page while connected.
  • LINE L VS LINE R sets the two halves side by side: capacity, rated, the BMS's own usable-capacity estimate, SoH, highest and lowest cell, spread and temperature. The bar is how they share the current, about half each; under 5 A it turns grey and keeps the last real split. If one line pulls away from the other for weeks, that line is ageing faster.
  • LIFETIME BMS STATISTICS: energy out and charged in over the pack's life, full cycles per line, and the lowest and highest cell voltage and temperature ever recorded. STORED CAPACITY: the eight capacities the battery keeps, highest first, the current one last.

"Points of charge went while parked"

When the car connects again after at least half an hour parked, and the charge has fallen more than 1.5 points with the odometer unchanged, a line at the top of the page says how much, once, until you press Dismiss. Climate left on, a timer or an open door are the usual causes; cold alone can move the gauge a point or two overnight. If it keeps happening with nothing switched on, have the car looked at.

Cells

The 96 cells of one line, eight to a row, numbered from the top left. LINE L and LINE R pick the line. Four buttons under it switch what each cell shows. The PACK MODULES °C card below gives the six module temperatures of each line, the hottest in amber, and says cold when the battery limits power until the pack passes about 13 °C.

  • VOLTS: which cell is highest and lowest right now. Blue is the lowest, red the highest, and both change with the load. On a healthy pack all 96 of a line are within a few millivolts of each other, so the colours rank the cells; they do not judge them. LOWEST CELL and HIGHEST CELL name them.
  • MILLIOHMS: each cell's resistance, and this one is a verdict. Green is normal, under 10 % above the middle of your own pack. Amber, 10 to 25 % above, is worth watching. Deep orange, 25 to 100 %, is a warning. Red, resistance doubled, is the published end-of-life line for a cell. Cells that stand out are listed as cards. The button stays grey until the current has moved by 20 A; a minute of ordinary driving with the page open does it, standing still never will. The whole pack reads higher in the cold and lower when warm, so compare a cell with its neighbours today, not with last week. Cells 9, 21, 33 and every twelfth after them can read high together while the current changes, mostly in the cold. That comes from how the car measures them, so they are not flagged; one of them high on its own still is.
  • CAPACITY: what each cell holds, as the battery has learned it. Compare the cells with each other rather than reading the number as the pack's size; the app says the scale is still being checked. The smallest cell sets what the whole pack can hold. SMALLEST, LARGEST and SPREAD (in %) sum it up; a few percent between smallest and largest is normal. It is asked for only while this view is open.
  • BALANCING: which cells the battery has had to balance most. Grey cells keep pace, brighter grey is more; amber to red are balanced less than a typical cell, the ones to look at. The button stays grey until the car's balance counters have been read with this page open.

Every cell suddenly reads far less in CAPACITY

Now and then, most often after a drive that takes the battery low, every cell drops together, on one car from about 41 Ah to 23 Ah, and stay there for a day or two before coming back on their own. The battery is re-learning its cells. It is not a fault and the pack's health does not move with it. Wait until the figures have settled before comparing cells.

History

  • HEALTH OVER TIME: the pack's Ah at each saved charge, against the distance driven, with the rated new capacity as the top line. LOSS/1000 KM and the figure 6,000 km ahead follow the average since new until three charges spread over 30 days give a trend of their own. MEASURED is what your own charges put in, on the dash's 0 to 100 % scale: the middle value of every saved charge that covered at least 30 points and was read from start to finish; CHARGES USED counts them. Compare it with the usable part of the pack, not with the 35.5 kWh of the whole pack, and expect it a few percent above: a charge puts in a little more than driving takes back out.
  • WEAK CELLS: how far the weakest cell has fallen behind a typical one, at each reading of the battery's counters, and when it started to. It needs a second reading after a few full charges.
  • LINE BALANCE OVER TIME: line R's share of the current at each saved charge. Each line should carry half.
  • CELL RESISTANCE (BMS) mΩ: the battery's own highest and lowest cell resistance, one sample a day, grouped by pack temperature. Compare a row with its own temperature band, not another. FIT L / R is the app's own figure from this visit when the load has moved enough.
  • CONTACTORS AND WIRING: how often the battery's main relays have closed (a drive normally counts one), and the resistance of the cell-sensing wiring harness, which should stay constant. A dealer check is due only if CHANGED rises and keeps rising.

The saved charges come from Charging; with Save charging sessions off, the history charts stay empty.

12 V

  • 12 V BATTERY: the small battery that runs the locks, lights and computers. With the car on, the DC/DC converter charges it and it reads about 14 V; about 12.5 V means it is not being charged. Parked, the car does not send it, so LAST READ says when it was last seen. DC/DC, DC/DC TARGET and DC/DC TEMP are the converter's load, target voltage and temperature.
  • BATTERY SENSOR: the car's own 12 V sensor, read once when the tab opens with the car on: charge, current, temperature, and its estimates of the voltage at rest and under load.
  • PARKED DRAIN: the car's record of its last five parks over an hour: how long, and the average draw in mA. A park still drawing over 0.2 A three hours in had something left on, or a unit that did not go to sleep. If it keeps happening with nothing left on, have the car checked: a flat 12 V battery stops the car starting.
  • VOLTAGE, LAST DRIVES: the 12 V reading from each recent drive, and the lowest it read with the DC/DC off at start-up and shutdown. A line that keeps sinking over months says the battery is weakening; have it tested.

While the page is open

  • The dashboard may pause. Each line's 96 cells come in one large reply, so with the drive log off the other readings wait until you leave the page. With the drive log on, everything keeps updating and the cells refresh less often instead. With Settings › Prioritize screen data on, every page reads only what it shows; an (i) in the top bar says when other readings are held back.
  • You do not have to watch it. Whenever the cells are read, a cell crossing into a band writes a line into the car log naming it, so a drive leaves a record with nobody looking.

Charging

Turn on Settings › Pages › Charging; it appears as the bolt in the bottom bar. It records every charge, open or not, and keeps the last one to look at afterwards. If you charge from a CHAdeMO socket, set Settings › Car › Charger port to CHAdeMO, or the charger's own values stay empty. Pictures: invented data.

During a charge

  • STATE OF CHARGE is the charge on the dash's scale, as a bar from 0 to 100 with a mark at the target. The chip beside the page title says AC (a plug) or DC (a charging post) as the car reports it.
  • POWER going into the battery, ADDED (the kWh put in so far) and TO 80 % with the clock time it should be done. Past 80 the target becomes 100 %. They come from how fast the charge rose over the last 5 minutes and say estimating until there is enough to go on. The car's own limit is not sent over the diagnostic port, so this is the app's estimate.
  • AVERAGE (kWh added over the time so far), ENERGY TO 80, INLET (the socket's temperature; two numbers when one pin runs hotter), and once the app can tell, FROM WALL (or FROM STATION on DC) and LOSS. If the inlet gets hot the page says to check the plug, the cable and the socket.
  • The car says the bulk charge is complete appears once the car's own bulk phase is over; expect it to charge slower to the end.
  • Under the curve: VOLTAGE, CURRENT and CAR SAYS, the car's own time to full. CHARGE DETAILS shows what the station offers, what the car asks for and LIMITED BY (station or car). On a DC charge WHO LIMITS IT shows charger max, what the car asks for and what is delivered.
  • LEFT / RIGHT LINES, PACK MODULES °C and PACK THERMAL: the pack's two halves side by side (amber where they differ), the module temperatures with the hottest in amber, and the coolant, pump and fan. A flame and a heater figure mean part of the power is heating the pack, not filling it; nothing to do.

The power curve

Power into the battery against charge time, or against charge level with SOC. A solid line is what the app read; a dashed line is a stretch where nobody was reading, filled in at an estimated power, and the chip says estimated. A dashed upright line marks where the car said bulk charge complete. Under it, while charging, HIGHEST CELL follows the fullest cell with the car's charge target and cell limit dashed: near full the car slows the charge to keep that cell under them. A weak cell shows first during a charge, running higher than the rest. A dropout of up to 5 minutes, or an app restart, carries on the same charge. A longer gap, or a charge that starts lower than the last one ended, starts a new one.

After a charge

Not charging, the page shows the last charge: ADDED, DURATION, AVERAGE, PEAK, ENDED (full, or stopped before or after the bulk charge was done), the inlet's highest temperature, the pack's temperature from start to end, how long it was heated, and what the station offered. A chip says Not reading the car or Cable in. With nothing saved yet it says it fills while the car takes a charge.

Saved charges

Charges are listed under SAVED CHARGES, newest first, grouped by month. A month's line shows its charges and how many were deleted; tap it to fold the month. Open months show ENERGY, AC and DC in kWh. Each charge shows when it ran, the kWh, how long, average and peak kW, an AC or DC chip, and the charge from and to. Turn on Settings › Logs and files › Save charging sessions (it appears once the Charging page is on) so each charge is kept as its own file. Tap a charge to draw it on the curve, tap again to go back. The three dots on a row open Share, Edit and Delete. Edit takes what the charger's receipt says: start, end, session time, energy, average; boxes left alone keep their value or follow from the others. Delete asks first and removes the file for good. Estimated means part of the charge was not read.

"Unfinished charge"

The car stopped charging while the adapter was asleep, so the log stops below where the car is now. If the charger told you when it finished, its peak or the kWh, type them in and press Finish with this; all three are optional. Discard deletes that charge's log, after asking.

"Charge not recorded" and "Charge not being read"

Charge not recorded: the car charged while the app was not reading it. It shows the charge it last saw, now, and about the kWh added; enter what the charger told you (all optional) and press Save as a charge, or Dismiss. Charge not being read: the car stopped answering during a charge, as it does when switched off or out of reach. Keep carries it on when the car answers again; End it now closes it at what was recorded.

TPMS

Turn on Settings › Pages › TPMS; it appears as the tyre in the bottom bar and takes effect on the next connection. The page stays upright when the phone is turned. Pictures: a real drive replayed.

The Honda e has no pressure sensors. A tyre losing air rolls on a slightly smaller radius and turns faster than the other three, and the car reports each wheel's speed. The page watches for that, the way the car's own deflation warning does. It also shows the car's own TPMS record.

Getting started

  • Set all four tyres cold with a gauge to the pressures on the driver's door pillar, enter them in bar, and press These are set - start learning.
  • Drive. With the page open, about two minutes of steady driving between 30 and 130 km/h teaches it these tyres. With the page closed, or under Android Auto, it keeps reading in the background and takes about ten minutes. The chip beside the title shows Learning with a percentage, and LEARNED counts the minutes; a short drive carries on where the last stopped.
  • "Not counting · speed / power / braking / cornering" names why a moment is skipped: speed outside 30-130 km/h, hard acceleration or regen, braking, or a corner.
  • "Waiting for wheel speeds" / "No wheel speeds yet" after switching the page on: disconnect and connect once. A car screen showing the dashboard pauses the wheel speeds while the page is open.

Reading it

  • The four tyres are drawn where they sit on the car. Each shows the pressure it would take to turn that fast, with a ±: the real value is inside it about 19 times in 20. Inside its ± it reads unchanged. Until there is an estimate a card shows the tyre's share of speed instead.
  • Amber is 10 % down, red 20 % down. Go and check that tyre with a gauge.
  • TREND, PER DRIVE: a small chart for each tyre, one point a drive for the last 60, the pressure you set dashed. A slow leak is one line sinking while the other three stay level.
  • After pumping the tyres, press I've set the pressures again and enter them, so it learns them afresh. If you reset TPMS at the dash, the page notices and relearns by itself.

The car's own TPMS

The CAR'S TPMS card lists what the car itself recorded: CALIBRATED and the ODOMETER at the last calibration, then each low-pressure warning or system fault, newest first, with the distance, temperature and cause. Warnings from before the last calibration are dimmed. The car stores the four wheel speeds at each warning; a wheel that read faster than the rest is the one to check first, and that tyre's card says car warned at N km. It shows even before you have entered pressures.

The chip at its top is the brake fluid level, read with the wheel speeds. If it says low, have the brakes checked before a long drive: low fluid comes with worn pads or a leak.

What it cannot see

All four going down together, since each tyre is compared with the others. It tells you to look, it is not a gauge: check the pressures by hand now and then. The explanation is in the Notes at the foot of the page.

Drive log

Turn on Settings › Pages › Drive log and allow location; it appears as the route icon in the bottom bar. Picture: invented data.

Each drive is saved as a GPX file, written as you drive, so it is complete even if the app closes on the way. A new file starts with each drive and after a charge; if the car or the adapter picks up again within five minutes, the same file carries on.

The list

  • At the top: DRIVES, how many are kept, and STORAGE, what they take on the phone. The share icon beside the title, Export all drives, sends every drive as a My Honda+ trips CSV.
  • By month, newest first. A month's line shows its name, its drives and how many were deleted; tap it to fold the month, or tap its share icon to export that month as a trips CSV. Under it, DISTANCE, ENERGY and CONSUMPTION for the month. Deleted drives stay in the month's totals and in its export.
  • Each drive: when it started and ended, the distance, how long it took, the average speed (both including stops, as the car's trip meter does), the energy that left the pack and what regen put back. Under it a bar shows the drive's consumption against its month's average, the tick: green at or under it, orange over.
  • A dash means the drive cannot say: consumption needs at least a kilometre, and the oldest drives have no used and regen figures.
  • RECORDING marks the drive being written. It has no menu and cannot be shared or deleted until it ends.
  • The three dots on a drive open Share, which sends the file to Drive, a mail or a map app, and Delete, which asks first and removes it from the phone for good.

The files

Each point carries the car's speed, power, current, pack voltage, the weakest cell's charge, energy used and regenerated, consumption, road grade and altitude; a map app ignores all that and draws the route. Settings › Logs and files › Extended driving data (it appears once the Drive log is on) adds the dash's charge, odometer, temperatures, range, battery health and the right line's 96 cell voltages, about 10 MB an hour, from the next app start. Open a file in the GPX Player to replay it. Drives stay on the phone until you delete them, unless Delete logs over 30 days is on in Settings (it needs a folder chosen).

Settings

The gear in the top bar, or the last page when swiping. Six sections: CAR, LOOK, PAGES, LOGS AND FILES, SHARING and ABOUT. Everything here is off until you turn it on, unless it says otherwise.

The first time

  1. Plug the OBD adapter in under the dashboard and switch the car on.
  2. Car › Adapter and pick yours from the list. If it says not paired yet, the app pairs it for you: confirm on the phone; a PIN is usually 1234 or 0000.
  3. Tap Not connected at the top left to connect.
  4. If your car has a CHAdeMO socket, set Car › Charger port to CHAdeMO.
  5. Logs and files › Folder › Choose folder, so drives and charges are kept where you can find them.
  6. Turn on the pages you want under Pages; each adds an icon to the bottom bar.

Car: adapter and connecting

The app reads the car through a Bluetooth OBD-II adapter (an ELM327 type) in the car's diagnostic socket. Adapter lists the Bluetooth devices in range: the likely adapters first, other devices under OTHER DEVICES, each marked Classic or Bluetooth LE. Most Bluetooth LE adapters never pair: pick them here, not in Android's Bluetooth settings. An empty list means switching the car on so the adapter powers up; an OBDLink MX+ is found only after its button has been pressed.

The dot at the top left shows the link; tap it to connect or disconnect.

It saysWhat it means
ConnectedThe car is answering and the readings are live.
ConnectingSetting up the adapter; a few seconds.
Car asleepThe adapter is connected but the car is not answering. A parked Honda e switches its modules off within a minute; open a door or switch the car on.
Adapter out of reachThe phone cannot find the adapter: usually the car is off, or the phone is too far away.
Not connectedNot trying. Tap it to connect.
DisconnectedThe link failed. See below.
  • Connect when the app opens connects every time you open the app, as if you had tapped it, except after you pressed Disconnect, until the app is closed and opened again.
  • Connect when the car does (Android 12 or newer) starts reading when the phone joins the car's Bluetooth, with the app closed. Pair the phone with the car as for calls, open the row and choose Link car, pick the car as the phone's Bluetooth list names it and confirm. Forget unlinks it. To have the route in the drive log on those drives, press Allow location all the time and choose that. Reading stops ten minutes after the car goes to sleep, so neither battery is drained overnight.
  • Charger port: CCS2 (World) or CHAdeMO (Japan). With the wrong one, rapid charges still show as DC with their power but the charger's own values stay empty. A change applies the next time the app connects.
  • Vehicle information and Fault codes are described below.
  • The adapter talks to one app at a time. If Car Scanner, ABRP's own OBD link or the adapter's app has it, this app gets nothing.
  • It finds the adapter but never connects: unplug the adapter for about ten seconds and plug it back in. Some adapters stop accepting anyone after a connection was cut off, while still showing up in the list.

Vehicle information and fault codes

Vehicle information has two tabs. CAR shows the brake-fluid warning when the level is low, the SERVICE minder, the speedometer's error worked out from steady driving, the battery's health and capacity and lifetime counters. MODULES holds the sheet of the car's identity as its modules report it: the VIN, each module's part, software and serial numbers. Press Read from car once while connected; the sheet is kept afterwards. Export as .txt saves all of it, for a dealer or your own records. Export anonymised .txt keeps each module's software version and the VIN with the car's serial starred out, enough to tell the model year but not the car: the one to post on a forum.

Fault codes shows the codes stored in each module, with a description where one is known. Press Read fault codes while connected. It cannot clear or turn off fault codes.

Look

  • Theme and colours opens its own page: MODE Auto, Dark (the default) or Light (Auto follows the phone's own light and dark setting, and on the car's screen it follows the car, which knows when the headlamps are on); the APP COLOUR; one colour per reading, the same on every screen, with dark and light each keeping their own; Min / avg / max line to hide the small line under a tile's number; and Reset all to defaults.
  • Columns in portrait / landscape: how many tiles fit across the dashboard; Auto picks from the screen. Minimum row height sets how tall a tile row is, with a hint how many rows fit.
  • Units: consumption in kWh/100km (the default), km/kWh, mi/kWh or kWh/100mi; the last two also switch distances and speeds to miles. It applies everywhere, except a dashboard tile that has been given its own unit in the editor.
  • Altitude: Sea level (as maps and other apps show it) or the raw GPS WGS84 ellipsoid, which reads some tens of metres higher.
  • Keep the screen on while the car is answering; the phone sleeps again when the car sleeps or the link drops. For a phone in a cradle, not in a pocket.
  • Prioritize screen data reads only what the open screen shows, so it updates faster. The other screens, the drive log, ABRP and the car's screen get nothing new meanwhile, and an (i) in the top bar says when that is happening. Off by default.

Pages

Each switch adds an icon to the bottom bar and a stop when swiping, in the order of the switches. Press and hold an icon, then drag, to move it.

  • Battery health (Battery health). While it is open the battery is the only thing asked for, so the dashboard's own readings pause, unless the car's screen is showing them or the drive log is on.
  • Charging (Charging). Costs nothing: it is built from readings the app takes anyway.
  • TPMS (TPMS). Reads the wheel speeds while the page is open, and in the background once your pressures are entered; takes effect on the next connection.
  • Drive log (Drive log) records each drive as a GPX file. Needs location allowed.
  • My Honda shows what the car last reported to Honda's servers, the ones the My Honda+ app reads, even when the adapter is out of reach. Once it is on, My Honda settings appears for signing in (Signed in / Not signed in).

Logs and files

  • Folder: open the row and Choose folder (later Change folder) to pick a folder on the phone for drives (GPX), saved charges and exports. A file is written privately while it is recorded, then copied into the folder and checked once it is finished. Without a folder, logs stay in the app's private storage; you can still share them, but a file manager will not show them. Open folder shows it, Forget lets go of it, Move logs brings older private logs across.
  • Save charging sessions (shown when Charging is on) keeps each charge as its own CSV file, listed on the Charging page.
  • Extended driving data (shown when Drive log is on) adds the dash's charge, odometer, temperatures, range, battery health and the right line's 96 cell voltages to each point: about 10 MB an hour, from the next app start.
  • Raw log and Share raw logs: every reply from the car, for the developer. Only when asked. The files are large, so turn it off when done.
  • Delete logs over 30 days (shown once a folder is chosen) removes drives and charges over 30 days old each time the app starts. With Drive upload on, only once they are in Drive, which keeps its copies; with it off they are gone for good.
  • Export settings saves the app's settings to a file; Import settings reads one back. Use it when moving to a new phone or reinstalling.
    • In the file: dashboard layouts, theme and colours, units, altitude, charger port, columns and row height, the pages and logs you turned on, keep-screen-on, prioritize, connect-when-the-app-opens, the ABRP settings and the vehicle information sheet.
    • Not in it: the adapter, the linked car and the storage folder. Pick those again after importing; after a reinstall, choose the folder first.
    • Keep the file private. It holds your ABRP token and key and the car's VIN.
  • Export dashboard writes only the tiles and their colours to a new dated file in your folder, to send to another driver. Import dashboard picks such a file: tick upright, sideways or both, and the theme colours only if you want the sender's theme too. It replaces only what you ticked and offers to back up first.

Sharing

  • Google Drive upload (only in builds that offer it) copies every log in the folder to a "Honda e logs" folder in your Drive, so they survive losing or resetting the phone. It needs the folder above. The app can see only the files it puts there, nothing else in your Drive. Upload to Drive now sends whatever is waiting.

ABRP

A Better Route Planner plans a trip with charging stops from your car's charge and consumption. Normally it guesses them from its own model. With Send live data on, this app feeds it the car's real readings, so the plan follows how the drive is actually going.

  1. In ABRP, open your car, Live data, add a generic connection, and copy its personal telemetry token.
  2. Create a free telemetry API key at abetterrouteplanner.com/home/app/api-keys/telemetry.
  3. In Settings › Sharing › A Better Route Planner turn Send live data on, then paste both into Personal telemetry token and Telemetry API key.

What is sent, every 5 seconds by default while the app is connected and the car is awake: charge, power, speed, position, heading and altitude (when location is allowed and Send the location is on), whether it is charging, AC or DC, parked or driving, battery health and capacity, pack voltage and current, battery and outside temperature, and the odometer. Send every can slow it to 10, 30 or 60 seconds, which means less data but ABRP fits consumption less well. Only to ABRP, and only while the switch is on.

  • Is it working? The page says Last sent 12:03:11, accepted, or refused: the car log says why: a wrong token or a missing key.
  • Use this or ABRP's own OBD connection, not both. The adapter talks to one app at a time.

About

Questions and answers is the in-app help. The ? beside a page's or a section's title opens it at that answer. Credits lists the sources and the car model's credit. The version and build are at the foot of the page.

Decoded, not official

Every reading in the app is decoded by the app itself from what the car sends, worked out from logs of real drives and charges, not from Honda's documentation. A value can be off, mean something slightly different from its label, or simply be wrong. Treat it as a guide: the car's own displays and warnings come first, and anything that matters for safety or a repair is for a Honda dealer to check.
